# Case Study Skill

Package AGLAYA client results into case studies that work as sales assets — concrete, numbered, and credible.

## Core Principle

A case study is not a success story. It is a proof document. The difference: a success story says "it went well." A case study says "here is the measurable state before, here is the measurable state after, and here is exactly what we built between them."

AGLAYA case studies are sales tools first and content second.

## Required Input

Before writing, gather:
- Client name (or anonymized identifier if confidentiality required)
- Industry and company size
- The specific problem that triggered the engagement
- What AGLAYA built (systems, automations, architecture)
- Measurable results: before and after metrics with numbers
- Timeline of the engagement
- Client's words about the result (if available)

If hard numbers are not available, flag this and ask. A case study without numbers is a testimonial. Do not confuse the two.

## Case Study Structure

### Headline
Lead with the result number. Not the client name.
- ✅ "90% Reduction in Unqualified Leads — POCURO Real Estate"
- ❌ "How POCURO Improved Their Marketing with AGLAYA"

### The Problem (2–3 short paragraphs)
- Describe the company and context (industry, size, relevant situation)
- Name the specific operational problem: what was happening manually, how long it took, what it cost
- Include the trigger: why they came to AGLAYA, what had failed before

### The Solution (2–3 short paragraphs)
- Describe what AGLAYA built (no jargon, business language)
- Explain the architecture in one sentence if relevant: "We built an automated qualification system that scores leads in real time against 12 criteria, eliminating the 3-day manual review cycle"
- Note the timeline from audit to live system

### The Results (numbers first)
Present as a results table or a tight list:
- Primary metric (the headline number)
- Secondary metrics (2–3 supporting numbers)
- Time to positive ROI
- Any qualitative result worth noting (team morale, client satisfaction, decision speed)

### The Quote (if available)
One sentence from the decision-maker. Specific and functional, not enthusiastic:
- ✅ "We went from a 3-day qualification process to real-time scoring. The team stopped doing manual work and started doing actual sales."
- ❌ "Working with AGLAYA has been a transformative experience for our company."

### The Takeaway (one paragraph)
One insight generalizable to other companies in similar situations. Not a pitch — an observation. "For real estate companies with high inbound lead volume, manual qualification is the single largest source of sales team friction. Automating it is not a technology decision; it is a capacity decision."

## Outputs to Produce

1. **Full Case Study** (400–600 words) — for website, PDF, proposal appendix
2. **LinkedIn Proof Post** (150–250 words) — for AGLAYA's company LinkedIn, leading with the headline number
3. **Social Proof Brief** — content for the client to post on their own channels as a market credential (positioning as tech-forward leader). Written from the client's perspective. Includes graphic text suggestion (minimalist) + copy (100–150 words)
4. **Newsletter/Intelligence Report** — B2B nurture asset. Subject line + pre-header (direct, data-led) + body (zero filler, zero empathy language, starts with the result)

## Confidentiality

If the client has not explicitly approved publication, default to anonymized version: "A real estate company in Colombia (80 employees, $3M annual revenue)." Only use full client name and logo when permission is confirmed.

## Tone

Clinical precision, not enthusiasm. The reader should feel like they're reading a business review, not a marketing brochure. Numbers do the convincing. Language stays out of the way.
