# Brand Voice Guidelines — AGLAYA

## The Voice in One Sentence

AGLAYA sounds like a surgeon who has already read the chart and is telling you what needs to happen — without padding, without apology, and without asking for permission to be right.

## Tone Spectrum

AGLAYA's tone sits at a specific point on several axes:

| Axis | Too far left | AGLAYA | Too far right |
|---|---|---|---|
| Directness | Passive/hedging | Surgical | Aggressive |
| Authority | Deferential | Confident | Arrogant |
| Warmth | Cold/clinical | Dry but human | Effusive |
| Complexity | Jargon-heavy | Precise | Oversimplified |
| Length | Verbose | Tight | Cryptic |

## The 10 Voice Rules

1. **Start with the point.** The first sentence is the most important. If it can be cut without loss, it should be.
2. **Numbers over claims.** "90% reduction" > "significant improvement". Always.
3. **Name what you won't do.** AGLAYA regularly states exclusions. This is confidence, not arrogance.
4. **No warm-up language.** "I hope this finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "Great question" — none of this.
5. **Use the client's language.** If they call it "monthly reporting", don't call it "business intelligence workflows".
6. **Passive voice dies.** "The system was built" → "We built". Active always.
7. **One idea per paragraph.** Especially on LinkedIn. White space is structure.
8. **No superlatives without proof.** "The best", "world-class", "revolutionary" — only with evidence.
9. **Uncomfortable when necessary.** If the client is doing something wrong, say so. Diplomatic but direct.
10. **End with force.** No "I look forward to hearing from you." End on a point, a question that demands an answer, or a clear next step.

## Words and Phrases AGLAYA Avoids

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| "Solutions" | Vague, overused |
| "Synergy" / "Synergies" | Corporate emptiness |
| "Leverage" (as a verb) | Consultant speak |
| "Disruptive" / "Innovative" | Said by everyone, means nothing |
| "Holistic" | Vague |
| "Best-in-class" | Unsubstantiated claim |
| "Passionate about" | Irrelevant to the client |
| "Partnership" | Implies equal contribution; use "engagement" or "collaboration" |
| "Excited to" | Hollow opener |
| "Value-add" | Buzzword |
| "Deep dive" | Overused |

## Content Formats by Channel

### AGLAYA LinkedIn Company Page
- **Frequency:** 3 posts/week
- **Language:** English (primary), Spanish (when content is LATAM-specific)
- **Length:** 150–350 words standard; 50–100 words for sharp takes
- **Format:** Short paragraphs, intentional white space, max 3 hashtags
- **Tone:** Institutional but not corporate. Firm but not distant.

### Website — Homepage
- **H1:** One strong claim (verb-first). "We build systems that make money while you sleep."
- **H2:** One specific claim. "For companies spending $15K+/month on work that should be automatic."
- **Body:** Tight paragraphs, no bullet points in prose sections
- **CTA:** Direct and filtered: "If your team does manual work that should be automated, let's map the cost."

### Website — About/Manifesto
- **Format:** Short declarative statements. Like a manifesto, not a bio.
- **Voice:** First person plural ("We") — institutional, not personal
- **Tone:** Convictions stated plainly. What AGLAYA believes. What AGLAYA will never do.

### Proposals
- **Language:** Match client's language
- **Tone:** The most restrained version of AGLAYA's voice. Precise, not provocative.
- **Format:** Business document with numbers up front

### Outreach (company email/LinkedIn)
- **Tone:** Curious and surgical. The prospect hasn't hired AGLAYA yet — don't act like they have.
- **Length:** As short as possible. Every sentence must serve the reply.
