## The headline: search demand is in ENGLISH/US, not Spanish

Phase 1 (qualitative) leaned Mexico because competitors are visible there. **Hard
numbers invert it.** Those Mexican competitors ride near-zero *search* volume
(they live on referrals/content/LinkedIn, not Google Search). The real,
capturable **search** demand is US/English — and it sits exactly on AGLAYA's
done-for-you wedge.

software" (5,000, Medio) which tilts SaaS/DIY.

## 🇲🇽 Mexico + 🇪🇸 Spain — Spanish search is too thin

Planner ranges for our candidate ES terms:
- `cuánto cuesta automatizar un proceso en una empresa` → **0–10/mo**
- `agencia de automatización para pymes` → **0–10/mo**
- `facturación automática cfdi` → **0–10/mo**
- `automatizar cobranza`, `automatizar reportes`, `cobranza automatizada` → **10–100/mo** (comp Bajo–Medio; e.g. cobranza automatizada CPC $5.74–15.00; automatizar reportes $1.61–3.58)

Too low for Google **Search** demand-first in either MX or ES. (Consistent with
the Phase-1 caveat that tools underreport B2B long-tail — but here even the mid
terms are 10–100, and the head/commercial terms are 0–10. This is genuinely thin,
not just undercounting.)

## Strategic implication

1. **Google Search Ads → US / English first.** Target the LOW-competition
   "…automation services/companies" + "rpa services company" + "automated document
   workflow" cluster (done-for-you wedge). Real demand, beatable auction.
2. **Spanish (MX/ES) ≠ Google Search.** Capturing search there = creating the
   category = expensive (the Scanner trap again). If we go LatAm/Spain, it must be
   **demand-GENERATION** (Meta/LinkedIn interruption, content, outbound), not
   search capture.
3. The site already speaks EN — the US/English play needs no new language, just
   positioning around the proven terms.
